## 中文版

### 一句话总结

最后一章是资源清单，作者推荐了线下社群、线上讨论、博客和书籍，核心意思是读书只是开始，真正重要的是去实践并加入身边的创业社群。

### 核心观点

1. **最重要的资源在本地。** Lean Startup 运动已经遍布全球，创业者不必非待在 Silicon Valley 才能找到同伴，但身处一个创业生态中仍然很重要。作者维护着本书的官方网站，上面有案例、延伸阅读和他的博客 Startup Lessons Learned 以及演讲材料。(p.274)

2. **社群和活动。** 写书时全球已有一百多个 Lean Startup meetup 小组，最大的在旧金山、波士顿、纽约、芝加哥和洛杉矶。此外还有志愿者维护的 wiki、由 Rich Collins 创办的 Lean Startup Circle 邮件列表（有数千名创业者交流），以及作者自己办的 Startup Lessons Learned 大会。(p.274-275)

3. **必读书和博客。** 作者把 Steve Blank 的 *The Four Steps to the Epiphany* 列为必读，这是 customer development 的奠基之作，他做 IMVU 时一直带在身边。其他推荐包括一本 customer development 入门书，以及一批创业博客，内容涉及增长指标、营销、病毒传播和融资。其中 Dave McClure 的 "Startup Metrics for Pirates" 对本书 engines of growth 的概念影响很大。(p.275-277)

4. **延伸阅读。** 书单大致分几类：Christensen 和 Moore 关于颠覆式创新和技术市场的著作；lean manufacturing 与 Toyota 相关的经典；Taylor、Ford、General Motors 等管理史；Deming、Drucker 的管理经典；Extreme Programming 等软件开发方法；以及商业模式调整方面的书。作者特别提到，Build-Measure-Learn 循环很大程度上借鉴了 John Boyd 的 OODA loop（Observe-Orient-Decide-Act）。(p.277-279)

### 关键术语

| 术语 | 解释 |
|---|---|
| Customer development | Steve Blank 提出的方法，强调在做产品的同时系统地了解客户 |
| OODA loop | John Boyd 提出的 Observe-Orient-Decide-Act 循环，是 Build-Measure-Learn 的灵感来源之一 |
| Community of practice | 围绕同一实践互相学习的社群，如 meetup 和线上讨论组 |

## English Version

### One-Sentence Summary

The final chapter is a resource guide covering local groups, online communities, blogs, and books, with the message that reading is only a start and the real value comes from practicing and joining a startup community near you.

### Key Points

1. **The most important resources are local.** The Lean Startup movement has spread worldwide, and entrepreneurs no longer need to be in Silicon Valley to find peers, though being part of a startup ecosystem still matters. Ries runs an official site for the book with case studies, further reading, his blog Startup Lessons Learned, and materials from his talks. (p.274)

2. **Communities and events.** At the time of writing there were more than a hundred Lean Startup meetup groups, the largest in San Francisco, Boston, New York, Chicago, and Los Angeles. There is also a volunteer-run wiki, the Lean Startup Circle mailing list founded by Rich Collins with thousands of entrepreneurs, and Ries's own Startup Lessons Learned conference. (p.274-275)

3. **Required reading and blogs.** Ries names Steve Blank's *The Four Steps to the Epiphany* as essential. It is the original book on customer development, and he carried a copy everywhere while building IMVU. He also recommends an introductory customer development book and a set of startup blogs on metrics, marketing, viral growth, and fundraising. Dave McClure's "Startup Metrics for Pirates" strongly shaped the book's idea of engines of growth. (p.275-277)

4. **Further reading.** The list covers a few groups: Christensen and Moore on disruptive innovation and technology markets, lean manufacturing and Toyota classics, management history on Taylor, Ford, and General Motors, management classics by Deming and Drucker, software methods like Extreme Programming, and a book on reworking business models. Ries notes that the Build-Measure-Learn loop owes a lot to John Boyd's OODA loop (Observe-Orient-Decide-Act). (p.277-279)

### Key Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Customer development | Steve Blank's method of systematically learning about customers alongside building the product |
| OODA loop | John Boyd's Observe-Orient-Decide-Act cycle, one inspiration for Build-Measure-Learn |
| Community of practice | A group that learns together around a shared practice, such as meetups and online forums |

### Discussion Questions

- What startup communities exist in your city or school? If you attended one, what question would you bring?
- How is the OODA loop similar to Build-Measure-Learn, and how is it different?
