# The Lean Startup, Chapter 2: Define (Who Is an Entrepreneur?)

> Part One: VISION ｜ PDF p.35-44

## 中文版

### 一句话总结

Startup 的定义和公司规模、行业无关，只要是在极度不确定的条件下创造新产品或服务，就是 startup。Intuit 的 SnapTax 和 TurboTax 说明，大公司也能像 startup 一样创新，前提是高层建立支持实验的系统。

### 核心观点

1. **大公司里也有创业者。** 作者在演讲中常遇到大公司的 general manager，他们懂组织政治，会设立有独立 P&L 的部门，也有远见和冒险精神。例如 Mark，他负责带领公司做一套基于互联网的新产品，读过 The Innovator's Dilemma，也不缺愿景。他觉得自己什么原材料都有，就是点不着火。(p.35-36)

2. **缺的是流程。** 传统管理理论把创新当作 black box，只关心要搭建什么样的组织结构。Mark 已经身处 black box 里面，他需要知道的是：团队建好之后该做什么、用什么流程、怎么对 milestones 负责。这正是 Lean Startup 要回答的问题。(p.36)

3. **Intrapreneur 和创业者本质相同。** 在大公司内部创业的人常被叫作 intrapreneurs。作者认为他们和其他创业者的共同点比大家想的多，所以书中的"entrepreneur"指整个 startup 生态，不分公司规模、行业和发展阶段。(p.36-37)

4. **Startup 的定义。** 作者把 startup 定义为：在极度不确定的条件下，为创造新产品或服务而设计的人类组织。这个定义最重要的地方在于它没提什么：不提公司大小、行业和经济部门。政府机构、VC 投资的公司、非营利组织里都可能有创业者。(p.37)

5. **拆解定义的每个部分。**
   - **Institution**：听起来像官僚体系，但成功的 startup 确实在做建组织的事，比如招人、协调工作、建立文化。
   - **Product**：取最宽的含义，客户和公司互动中体验到的一切都算产品。
   - **Innovation**：同样取宽义，包括科学发现、把旧技术用到新用途、新的 business model，或者把产品带到新地区、新客户群。
   - **Extreme uncertainty**：完全复制一家现有业务的新店也许是好投资，但不是 startup，因为成败只取决于执行，风险可以被准确评估，所以这类小生意靠银行贷款就能融资。(p.37-38)

6. **SnapTax 的故事。** 2009 年，一个团队想让人不用去报税店。他们发现很多人不会用扫描仪，于是让用户用手机拍 W-2 表格，测试中用户又问能不能直接在手机上报完税。第一版只支持报税很简单的用户，而且只限加州，从一张照片就能完成大部分 1040 EZ。2011 年全国发布后，头三周下载量超过 35 万。(p.39)

7. **做出 SnapTax 的是 Intuit。** Intuit 有 7700 多名员工，年收入数十亿美元。这个团队领全薪、在正常办公室上班，但他们就是创业者。SnapTax 还直接和自家的 TurboTax 竞争。大公司通常擅长 sustaining innovation，也就是改进现有产品，却难以做出 disruptive innovation。(p.39-40)

8. **SnapTax 成功的条件。** 团队没有从外部挖明星创业者，而是从公司内部组建；高层给了他们一块可以自由实验的"island of freedom"；团队一开始只有五个人。作者的结论是：创新虽然自下而上、难以预测，但可以被管理，而培养创业精神是高层管理者的责任。(p.40-41)

9. **Intuit 为什么要变。** Scott Cook 在 1983 年和 Tom Proulx 创办 Intuit。到 2002 年，Cook 统计了公司十年来所有新产品的数据，发现投入巨大、回报很低，太多新产品失败了。他意识到公司一直使用的管理范式无法应对持续创新的需要。(p.41)

10. **TurboTax 从一次大赌注变成几百次实验。** 过去 TurboTax 团队一年只做一个大项目，赶在报税季上线。现在他们在两个半月的报税季里测试 500 多个改动，每周最多 70 个测试，周四上线，周末运行，周一看结果，周二开始下结论，下个周四再上新测试。Cook 认为，只有一次测试机会时，大家只能靠说服别人来推销想法，组织里就全是政客；测试多了，每个人的想法都能跑一跑，才会培养出创业者。(p.42)

11. **阻力来自管理层。** Cook 指出，团队本身很愿意快速上线、让客户投票，真正的问题在领导和中层，很多人靠分析和做计划取得成功。作者认为，市场领先地位能维持的时间越来越短，大公司唯一可持续的增长方式是建立一个用 Lean Startup 方法持续产出 disruptive innovation 的 "innovation factory"。(p.43)

12. **Intuit 怎么衡量创新。** CEO Brad Smith 在作者向全体 7000 多名员工演讲后说，Intuit 符合 startup 定义的全部三个部分。他们用两个指标考核：使用三年前还不存在的产品的客户数，以及来自这类产品的收入占比。过去一个成功新产品平均要 5.5 年才做到 5000 万美元收入，而最近一年里，上线不到十二个月的产品合计就带来了 5000 万美元。(p.43-44)

13. **领导的任务是建系统。** TurboTax 能做 500 个实验，是因为公司投资了能快速修改网站、部署和分析测试的系统。Cook 的看法是，领导者不应该像凯撒那样对每个想法竖拇指或倒拇指，而应该建立文化和系统，让团队以实验系统的速度前进。(p.44)

### 关键术语

| 术语 | 解释 |
|---|---|
| Startup | 在极度不确定的条件下，为创造新产品或服务而设计的人类组织 |
| Intrapreneur | 在大公司内部创业的人 |
| Extreme uncertainty | startup 所处的环境，结果无法靠历史数据准确预测 |
| Sustaining innovation | 对现有产品的渐进改进，服务现有客户 |
| Disruptive innovation | 能开辟新增长来源的突破性新产品 |
| Island of freedom | 高层为创新团队划出的、可以自由实验的空间 |
| Innovation factory | 用 Lean Startup 方法持续产出创新的组织能力 |
| Fast-cycle testing | 短周期、大量的测试，例如 TurboTax 每周几十个实验 |

## English Version

### One-Sentence Summary

A startup is defined by extreme uncertainty, not by size or industry. Intuit's SnapTax and TurboTax show that a large company can innovate like a startup when senior leaders build systems that support experimentation.

### Key Points

1. **Entrepreneurs exist inside big companies too.** At his talks, Ries often meets general managers from large companies who understand corporate politics, can set up divisions with their own P&L, and have vision and appetite for risk. Mark is one example. He was leading a new line of internet products, had read The Innovator's Dilemma, and had a clear vision. He felt he had all the raw materials but could not get the fire started. (p.35-36)

2. **What's missing is a process.** Traditional management theory treats innovation as a black box and focuses on which structures to set up. Mark was already inside the box. He needed to know what the team should do once it existed, what process to follow, and how to be held accountable to milestones. Those are the questions the Lean Startup is built to answer. (p.36)

3. **Intrapreneurs are entrepreneurs.** People who build startups inside established companies are sometimes called intrapreneurs. Ries finds they have much in common with other entrepreneurs, so in this book "entrepreneur" covers the whole startup ecosystem regardless of company size, sector, or stage. (p.36-37)

4. **Defining a startup.** Ries defines a startup as a human institution designed to create a new product or service under conditions of extreme uncertainty. The key is what the definition leaves out: company size, industry, and sector. Entrepreneurs can work in a government agency, a venture-backed company, or a nonprofit. (p.37)

5. **Each part of the definition.**
   - **Institution**: the word suggests bureaucracy, yet successful startups do institution-building work such as hiring, coordinating, and shaping culture.
   - **Product**: used broadly. Anything customers experience in dealing with the company counts.
   - **Innovation**: also broad. It includes scientific discoveries, new uses for existing technology, new business models, and bringing a product to a new place or an underserved group.
   - **Extreme uncertainty**: an exact clone of an existing business may be a good investment, but it is not a startup, because success depends only on execution and can be modeled accurately. That is why many small businesses can get simple bank loans. (p.37-38)

6. **The SnapTax story.** In 2009, a team wanted to free people from expensive tax stores. They found many people could not use their scanners, so they let customers photograph their W-2 forms with a phone. During testing, customers asked whether they could finish the whole return on the phone. The first version only handled simple returns and only in California, completing most of a 1040 EZ from one photo. After the 2011 nationwide launch, it passed 350,000 downloads in three weeks. (p.39)

7. **SnapTax was built by Intuit.** Intuit had more than 7,700 employees and billions in annual revenue. The team earned full salaries and worked in a regular office, and they were still entrepreneurs. SnapTax even competed with Intuit's own TurboTax. Large companies are usually good at sustaining innovation, improving existing products, and bad at disruptive innovation. (p.39-40)

8. **Why SnapTax worked.** The team came from inside Intuit rather than from outside hires. Executive sponsors gave them an "island of freedom" to experiment. They started with five people. Ries concludes that innovation is bottom-up and unpredictable but can still be managed, and that cultivating entrepreneurship is senior management's job. (p.40-41)

9. **Why Intuit needed to change.** Scott Cook founded Intuit in 1983 with Tom Proulx. In 2002, Cook reviewed ten years of data on Intuit's new product launches and found poor returns on large investments, with too many failures. He concluded that the company's management paradigm could not handle continuous innovation. (p.41)

10. **TurboTax went from one big bet to hundreds of tests.** TurboTax used to plan one major initiative a year for tax season. Now it tests over 500 changes in a two-and-a-half-month season, up to 70 tests a week: launch Thursday, run over the weekend, read results Monday, draw conclusions from Tuesday, and launch new tests the next Thursday. Cook's view is that with only one test, people must sell their ideas, so the company fills with politicians. With hundreds of tests, everyone's ideas get a run, and that develops entrepreneurs. (p.42)

11. **Resistance comes from managers.** Cook says teams love shipping fast and letting customers decide. The real obstacle is leaders and middle managers who succeeded through analysis and planning. Ries argues that market leadership lasts less and less time, so an established company's only sustainable path to growth is an "innovation factory" that uses Lean Startup techniques to produce disruptive innovations continuously. (p.43)

12. **How Intuit measures innovation.** After Ries spoke to all 7,000-plus employees, CEO Brad Smith told them Intuit matched all three parts of the startup definition. Intuit tracks two numbers: customers using products that did not exist three years earlier, and the share of revenue from such products. Under the old model, a successful new product took an average of 5.5 years to reach $50 million in revenue. In the past year, offerings less than twelve months old brought in $50 million combined. (p.43-44)

13. **Leaders build the system.** TurboTax could run 500 experiments because Intuit invested in systems for quickly changing the website and building, deploying, and analyzing tests. Cook says leaders should stop acting like Caesar, giving each idea a thumbs up or down, and instead build the culture and systems that let teams move at the speed of experimentation. (p.44)

### Key Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Startup | A human institution designed to create a new product or service under conditions of extreme uncertainty |
| Intrapreneur | Someone building a startup inside an established company |
| Extreme uncertainty | The condition startups face, where outcomes cannot be predicted from past data |
| Sustaining innovation | Incremental improvements to existing products for existing customers |
| Disruptive innovation | Breakthrough products that open new sources of growth |
| Island of freedom | Protected space leaders create so a team can experiment |
| Innovation factory | An organization's ability to produce innovations continuously with Lean Startup methods |
| Fast-cycle testing | Running many short tests, such as TurboTax's dozens per week |
