## 中文版

### 一句话总结

创业也需要管理，但传统管理方法不适合高度不确定的环境。Lean Startup 提供了一套专门为创业设计的管理方法，核心是用 validated learning 来衡量进展。

### 核心观点

1. **创业就是管理。** 建立一家 startup 本质上是在建立一个组织，所以一定需要管理。很多创业者担心管理会带来官僚主义，于是干脆什么流程都不要，结果往往是混乱而不是成功。作者说自己早期的几次创业失败都属于这种情况。(p.24)

2. **机会很多，浪费也很多。** 现代管理和技术让生产力大幅提升，美国制造业就业在减少，产出却在增长。所以现在创业的人比历史上任何时候都多。但因为缺少一套适合新业务的管理方法，大量产品上市几周就下架，或者根本没人用。作者认为最大的浪费是人的时间、热情和能力。(p.24-25)

3. **名字来自 Toyota 的 lean manufacturing。** Lean 思想包括：发挥一线员工的知识、缩小 batch size、just-in-time 生产、加快周期，以及区分创造价值的活动和浪费。Lean Startup 把这些思想搬到创业中，但换了一个衡量进展的单位：制造业看产出的实物，创业看 validated learning（第 3 章详细讲）。(p.28)

4. **好的创业理论要能做出可检验的预测。** 作者举例：如果公司改用 cross-functional teams，并用 learning milestones 来考核，而不是按职能部门考核，员工很快会抱怨效率下降。原因是大家习惯用"今天做了多少活"来衡量自己，而学习是看不见摸不着的。但对 startup 来说，按时按预算做出一个没人要的产品没有意义，真正的目标是尽快找出客户想要、愿意付钱的东西。(p.29-30)

5. **开车，而不是发射火箭。** 作者用汽车做比喻，汽车有两个 feedback loop：
   - **发动机内部的循环**，对应 startup 的 *engine of growth*。每次产品更新、新功能、新营销活动，都是在调整这台发动机，这个过程叫 *tuning the engine*。
   - **司机和方向盘之间的循环**，对应 *Build-Measure-Learn* feedback loop。司机随时根据路况调整方向。

   火箭则需要在发射前把每一步都算好，一点小误差就可能导致灾难。很多 business plan 就像火箭发射计划，假设一错，全盘皆输。(p.30-31)

6. **"Achieved failure"。** 作者合作过的一家公司预测新产品会有几百万用户，于是大张旗鼓地发布，并提前投入大量基础设施、招人和客服。结果用户没来，公司投入太深，来不及调整。他们完美执行了一个本身就错误的计划。(p.31-32)

7. **Vision, Strategy, Product 三层结构。**
   - **Vision**（愿景）：startup 的 true north，几乎不变。
   - **Strategy**（战略）：包括 business model、产品路线图、对合作伙伴和竞争对手的看法、目标客户是谁。偶尔需要改变，这就是 *pivot*。
   - **Product**（产品）：战略的最终产物，通过持续优化不断变化。

   遇到挫折时，愿景不变，调整的是战略或产品。(p.32-33)

8. **Startup 是一个活动组合（portfolio of activities）。** 同时在做三件事：让发动机运转（获客和服务现有客户）、调优（改进产品、营销、运营）、掌舵（决定是否 pivot）。小公司和大公司都要平衡这三者，区别只是比例不同。(p.34)

9. **大公司里的创新者也是创业者。** 想象一位大公司经理一年后向 CFO 汇报："增长目标没达成，几乎没有新客户和收入，但我们学到了很多，再给我一年。"在传统管理里，这等于宣告失败，因为没有结果就意味着计划不周或执行不力。但新产品开发恰恰经常需要经历这种"失败"。这些 intrapreneurs 同样需要 entrepreneurial management，这是第 2 章的主题。(p.34)

### 关键术语

| 术语 | 解释 |
|---|---|
| Entrepreneurial management | 专门为高度不确定环境设计的管理方法 |
| Lean manufacturing | Toyota 的生产方法，由 Taiichi Ohno 和 Shigeo Shingo 发展，核心是消除浪费 |
| Validated learning | Lean Startup 衡量进展的单位，用实验证明学到了关于客户的真实信息 |
| Engine of growth | startup 实现可持续增长的机制（第 10 章详细讲） |

| Intrapreneur | 在大公司内部做创新的人 |

### 我的思考题

- 你见过哪些"achieved failure"的例子？计划执行得很好，但方向错了。
- 在你实习或工作过的组织里，考核方式更像"按职能部门看产出"，还是"按学习成果"？
- Vision 和 strategy 的边界在哪里？什么情况下改变的其实是 vision，而不只是一次 pivot？

## English Version

### One-Sentence Summary

Startups need management, but traditional management does not fit extreme uncertainty. The Lean Startup offers a management method built for entrepreneurship, and it measures progress through validated learning.

### Key Points

1. **Entrepreneurship is management.** Building a startup means building an institution, so it requires management. Many founders avoid process altogether because they fear bureaucracy, and that usually leads to chaos. Ries says his own early startup failures were of this kind. (p.24)

2. **Huge opportunity, huge waste.** Modern management and technology have raised productivity so much that U.S. manufacturing output keeps growing while manufacturing jobs decline. More people are starting companies than ever before. Without a management approach suited to new ventures, though, many products are pulled weeks after launch or never get used. Ries sees the biggest loss as wasted human time, passion, and skill. (p.24-25)

3. **The name comes from Toyota's lean manufacturing.** Lean thinking includes using frontline workers' knowledge, shrinking batch sizes, just-in-time production, faster cycle times, and separating value-creating work from waste. The Lean Startup applies these ideas to entrepreneurship with a different unit of progress. Manufacturing counts physical goods. Startups count validated learning (covered in Chapter 3). (p.28)

4. **A good theory of entrepreneurship makes testable predictions.** Ries's example: if a company switches to cross-functional teams held accountable to learning milestones instead of functional departments, people will soon complain that productivity has dropped. They are used to judging a good day by how much work they finished, and learning feels intangible. For a startup, building something nobody wants on time and on budget is pointless. The real goal is to find out what customers want and will pay for, as fast as possible. (p.29-30)

5. **Drive a car, don't launch a rocket.** A car runs on two feedback loops:
   - **The loop inside the engine** maps to a startup's *engine of growth*. Every product update, feature, or marketing campaign is an attempt to improve it. Ries calls this *tuning the engine*.
   - **The loop between driver and steering wheel** maps to the *Build-Measure-Learn* feedback loop. The driver keeps adjusting to the road.

   A rocket must have every step calculated before launch, and a tiny error can be catastrophic. Many business plans are written like rocket launches, so one wrong assumption can sink everything. (p.30-31)

6. **"Achieved failure."** One company Ries worked with forecast millions of users for a new product. It launched with a big splash and invested heavily in infrastructure, hiring, and support ahead of demand. The customers never came, and the company was too committed to adjust in time. It executed a flawed plan perfectly. (p.31-32)

7. **Vision, strategy, product.**
   - **Vision**: the startup's true north. It rarely changes.
   - **Strategy**: the business model, product road map, view of partners and competitors, and target customer. It changes occasionally, and that change is a *pivot*.
   - **Product**: the end result of the strategy. It changes constantly through optimization.

   When setbacks happen, the vision stays and the strategy or product adjusts. (p.32-33)

8. **A startup is a portfolio of activities.** It runs the engine (acquiring and serving customers), tunes it (improving product, marketing, operations), and steers (deciding whether to pivot), all at once. Small and large companies both balance these three. Only the mix differs. (p.34)

9. **Innovators inside big companies are entrepreneurs too.** Picture a manager telling the CFO after a year: "We missed our growth targets and have almost no new customers or revenue, but we learned a lot. Give us another year." In general management, missing results means poor planning or poor execution. Yet new product development often requires this kind of failure along the way. These intrapreneurs also need entrepreneurial management, which is the topic of Chapter 2. (p.34)

### Key Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Entrepreneurial management | Management designed for conditions of extreme uncertainty |
| Lean manufacturing | Toyota's production system, developed by Taiichi Ohno and Shigeo Shingo, focused on eliminating waste |
| Validated learning | The Lean Startup's unit of progress: evidence from experiments about what customers really want |
| Engine of growth | The mechanism a startup uses to achieve sustainable growth (Chapter 10) |
| Build-Measure-Learn | Build something, measure how customers respond, learn, and decide what to do next |
| Pivot | A major change in strategy while keeping the vision |
| Learning milestones | Checkpoints that judge teams by what they learned, not by output volume |
| Intrapreneur | Someone who drives innovation inside an established company |

### Discussion Questions

- Can you think of an "achieved failure," where a plan was executed well but the direction was wrong?
- In organizations you have worked in, were people evaluated by functional output or by learning?
- Where is the line between vision and strategy? When does a change go beyond a pivot and alter the vision itself?
